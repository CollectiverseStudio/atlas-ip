## HOT TAKES ON COLLECTING (Posts 28–35)

### Post 28
Hot take: 90% of collections are underinsured.

Your homeowner's policy caps collectibles at $1,500-$2,500 total.

If you have more than that (you probably do), you're one incident away from a devastating loss.

Document everything. Insure properly.

### Post 29
Hot take: the "collectibles bubble" didn't burst.

Speculation left.
Collectors stayed.
The market normalized.

There's a difference between a crash and a correction. The former kills markets. The latter strengthens them.

### Post 30
Hot take: raw cards > slabbed cards for personal collections.

Yes, I said it. Fight me in the comments.

(Slabbing for selling/investment makes sense. But for YOUR collection? Hold that card. Feel the edges. Display it YOUR way.)

### Post 31
Hot take: if you can't quickly prove what you own, you don't truly own it.

No documentation = no insurance claim.
No provenance = no premium at sale.
No records = no legacy for your family.

Ownership without proof is just possession.

### Post 32
Hot take: the "what's it worth?" question is wrong.

Better questions:
• What did the last 5 actually SELL for?
• What condition is mine relative to those?
• Is demand rising or falling?
• How quickly do these sell?

Context > number. Always.

### Post 33
Hot take: the best collection isn't the most expensive one.

It's the most intentional one.

A focused collection of $20 items you love will bring more joy than a scattered collection of "investments" you don't connect with.

Collect with purpose.

### Post 34
Hot take: collecting is financial literacy education that nobody talks about.

Research. Valuation. Market timing. Condition assessment. Authentication. Supply and demand.

Kids learning to collect are learning to invest. Without the risk of actual markets.

### Post 35
Hot take: most "collection management apps" are just spreadsheets with a coat of paint.

Real collection management means AI identification, provenance tracking, market intelligence, estate planning, and community.

Not a prettier Excel.
