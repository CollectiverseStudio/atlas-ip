# Collectiverse Character Rendering and Animation Rules

## Purpose

This document defines production rules for rendering, animating, illustrating, prompting, licensing, and merchandising Collectiverse characters.

These rules apply to Atlas, the hero team, villains, supporting characters, future character variants, comic strips, short animations, stickers, social media content, educational graphics, app onboarding, merchandise, and promotional material.

## Core Rule

Every Collectiverse character must remain recognizable by silhouette, role, personality, and core visual identity even when simplified.

A viewer should be able to identify the character from:

- A small app icon
- A black silhouette
- A sticker
- A toy prototype
- A comic panel
- A social media thumbnail
- A simple line drawing
- A 3D render
- A children’s coloring page

## Style Direction

Collectiverse characters should feel:

- Original
- Friendly
- Collectible
- Trustworthy
- Expressive
- Modern
- Story-driven
- Safe for families
- Useful for adult collectors
- Distinct from existing famous IP

The overall style should be clean, dimensional, and readable. Avoid excessive realism, harsh horror, generic superhero styling, or imitation of any known animation studio or franchise.

## Proportion Rules

Hero characters should generally use approachable stylized proportions:

- Larger heads than realistic humans.
- Expressive eyes.
- Softened geometry.
- Clear hands and gestures.
- Strong readable footwear or lower-body grounding.
- Distinct torso symbol or role-based accessory.

Villains may use sharper or more asymmetrical proportions, but must remain appropriate for a family-friendly brand.

## Silhouette Rules

Each character must have a unique silhouette.

Silhouette anchors:

- Atlas: rounded explorer mascot, chest emblem, gloves, boots.
- Pixel: small rounded-square digital companion.
- Ink: slim flowing storyteller with writing/document cues.
- Sterling: tall refined appraiser with elegant posture.
- Forge: broad sturdy protector with large gloves.
- Porter: practical logistics guardian with straps and package cues.
- Echo: slim signal/provenance figure with soft rings or glow.
- The Director: tall controlled theatrical mastermind.
- The Counterfeiter: average, asymmetrical, almost-correct false identity.
- The Archivist: tall thin record-hoarder with locked paper layers.
- The Broker: compact polished predatory dealmaker.
- The Smuggler: lean concealed broken-custody mover.
- The Restorer: elegant too-perfect undisclosed alteration figure.
- The Hacker: twitchy angular digital-corruption figure.
- The Hoarder: large burdened cluttered possessor.

## Eye Rules

Eyes carry most of the emotional readability.

Heroes:

- Clear eyes.
- Warm or curious expressions.
- Strong eyebrow acting.
- No dead stares.
- No sinister glow.

Villains:

- More controlled, narrow, mismatched, hidden, or unstable eyes depending on character.
- Must still be expressive.
- Should not become graphic horror.

Atlas must always have the warmest and most emotionally accessible eyes.

## Hand Rules

Hands matter because Collectiverse is about handling, protecting, scanning, documenting, moving, and authenticating collectibles.

Hero hands should usually look careful, gloved, and safe.

Villain hands should reveal threat:

- The Counterfeiter: quick, label-switching hands.
- The Broker: contract and handshake hands.
- The Smuggler: hidden hands.
- The Restorer: too-precise hands.
- The Hacker: twitchy digital hands.
- The Hoarder: clutching hands.

Do not show heroes mishandling collectibles.

## Color Rules

Colors should support character role.

Heroes use trust, clarity, warmth, and role-based accents.

Villains use suspicion, concealment, over-polish, artificial perfection, shadow, or corruption.

Avoid random palette changes. Alternate outfits must preserve the character’s core identity.

## Texture Rules

Use texture to communicate role:

- Atlas: clean adventure fabric and matte surfaces.
- Pixel: smooth glassy digital accents.
- Ink: paper, ink, label, and archival textures.
- Sterling: brushed metal, velvet, and refined inspection tools.
- Forge: reinforced matte protection surfaces.
- Porter: canvas straps, shipping labels, and padded gear.
- Echo: subtle glow and translucent signal edges.
- Villains: fake gloss, dust, hidden labels, sterile perfection, corrupted screen texture, or clutter.

Textures must not make characters visually muddy.

## Animation Rules

### Atlas

Atlas moves with curious bounce and purposeful energy. He leans into discovery, points clearly, and handles items carefully.

### Pixel

Pixel hops, zips, scans, blinks, and reacts quickly. Pixel’s digital effects must not overpower the character.

### Ink

Ink moves smoothly and expressively, often drawing shapes in the air or carefully handling records.

### Sterling

Sterling moves slowly and precisely. Small gestures carry authority.

### Forge

Forge moves heavily and protectively. Motions are deliberate and careful.

### Porter

Porter moves efficiently, scans, checks, carries, routes, and secures.

### Echo

Echo moves quietly and gracefully, with subtle signal-ring or provenance-thread effects.

### Villains

Villain movement must reflect threat:

- The Director: controlled, theatrical, minimal.
- The Counterfeiter: quick, nervous, slippery.
- The Archivist: slow, dry, locked, then suddenly sharp.
- The Broker: fast, persuasive, invasive.
- The Smuggler: silent, concealed, route-focused.
- The Restorer: graceful, sterile, precise.
- The Hacker: twitchy, glitchy, abrupt.
- The Hoarder: slow, heavy, protective, burdened.

## Expression Library Requirement

Every character should eventually receive visual model sheets for:

- Neutral
- Smile
- Laugh
- Shock
- Confusion
- Suspicion
- Thinking
- Determination
- Concern
- Anger
- Embarrassment
- Pride
- Fear
- Tiredness

Atlas should receive the most complete expression library first.

## Pose Library Requirement

Every character should eventually receive pose sheets for:

- Standing neutral
- Walking
- Running or moving quickly
- Looking up
- Looking down
- Pointing
- Holding a collectible
- Inspecting an item
- Reacting to bad news
- Celebrating a discovery
- Teaching a collector
- Facing their opposite hero or villain

## AI Image Generation Rules

When generating AI images of Collectiverse characters:

1. Use the relevant character profile as the primary source.
2. Do not reference famous characters or famous studios.
3. Do not ask for style imitation of known IP.
4. Preserve the silhouette, role, expression, and personality.
5. Keep collectibles handled safely.
6. Avoid real company marks unless separately licensed.
7. Avoid real grading-company logos in fictional scenes.
8. Use fictional labels when a grading or certification mark is needed.
9. Do not create visual designs that weaken Collectiverse ownership.
10. Keep Atlas visually consistent across all outputs.

## Comics Rules

Collectiverse comics should be clear, educational, and character-driven.

Preferred comic functions:

- Explain collecting concepts.
- Warn about fraud.
- Teach preservation.
- Show marketplace trust.
- Make pricing less intimidating.
- Introduce Collectiverse features.
- Build emotional connection to Atlas and the team.

Comics should avoid cruelty, mockery of collectors, excessive sarcasm, or adult-only humor.

## Merchandise Rules

Characters must remain readable on:

- Stickers
- Shirts
- Hats
- Pins
- Plush toys
- Trading cards
- Posters
- App badges
- Convention banners
- Packaging
- Educational inserts

Simplified merchandise versions may reduce small texture details but must preserve silhouette, eyes, core colors, and role symbols.

## Brand Protection Rules

Never do the following:

- Make Atlas resemble a famous existing character.
- Turn Pixel into a generic robot mascot.
- Make Forge a weapon-based fighter.
- Make The Hacker provide real hacking instructions.
- Use real grading-company logos without permission.
- Use real collectible images in ways that create copyright risk without review.
- Present villain behavior as recommended practice.
- Make Collectiverse characters visually interchangeable with external IP.
- Change character roles casually.
- Create unauthorized alternate canon.

## Canon Expansion Rules

New characters may be added if they represent a real Collectiverse concept, such as:

- Estate planning
- Marketplace dispute resolution
- Authentication science
- Card show discovery
- Digital asset custody
- Insurance
- Community trust
- Creator royalties
- Public catalog research

Every new character must have:

- Clear role
- Unique silhouette
- Distinct personality
- Physical description
- Strengths
- Weaknesses
- Relationship to Atlas
- Brand-safe villain or hero function
- No confusing similarity to existing characters

## Final Standard

A Collectiverse character is successful when the viewer can answer these questions immediately:

1. Who is this?
2. What do they do in Collectiverse?
3. Are they trustworthy or dangerous?
4. What collectible-world concept do they represent?
5. Why could only Collectiverse own this character?
