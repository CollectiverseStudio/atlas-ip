# Ink Character Profile

## Role

Ink is the documentation, labeling, storytelling, and catalog specialist. Ink represents written history, provenance records, checklists, descriptions, notes, signatures, certificates, and the human stories attached to collectibles.

## Physical Appearance

Ink is slightly shorter than Atlas and has a flowing creative silhouette. Ink looks like a living blend of archivist, illustrator, notebook, and label-maker. The body is rounded and soft, with subtle paper, canvas, or label texture. The head may have a gentle taper inspired by a drop of ink or pen nib, but must remain original and friendly.

Ink's eyes are thoughtful, expressive, and slightly dreamy. The eyebrows are soft and flexible. The smile is gentle and knowing, like someone who remembers the story behind every object. Ink's hands are graceful and precise, capable of writing, drawing, tagging, and carefully turning pages.

Ink may carry a small notebook, tag roll, pen tool, label strip, or crossbody pouch. Accessories should feel integrated, not cluttered. Surfaces may include paper grain, stitched patches, printed label edges, or ink-line accents.

## Personality and Behavior

Ink is thoughtful, poetic, organized, and emotionally intelligent. Ink believes the story of a collectible matters as much as the price. Ink asks: Who owned it? Where did it come from? Why was it saved? What memory is attached to it?

Ink moves smoothly and deliberately. Ink taps a pen against the chin, flips through notes, sketches labels in midair, and becomes dramatically concerned when provenance is missing.

## Strengths

Ink is excellent at descriptions, provenance, collection notes, estate stories, labels, records, and transforming messy information into meaningful history.

## Weaknesses

Ink can over-document and slow down fast workflows. Ink can become sentimental and may resist letting go of objects with strong stories.

## Never Do

Ink must never become a generic pen mascot, bookworm stereotype, or office-supply character.
