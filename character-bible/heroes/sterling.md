# Sterling Character Profile

## Role

Sterling is the appraisal, valuation, condition, and market intelligence specialist. Sterling represents careful judgment, fair pricing, quality, and disciplined collector confidence.

## Physical Appearance

Sterling is taller and more elegant than Atlas. Sterling's silhouette is upright, composed, and refined. The body is slim but strong, with excellent posture and controlled movement. Sterling should feel like a premium appraiser without looking snobbish, corporate, or old-fashioned.

Sterling has sharp but kind eyes, precise eyebrows, and a calm mouth. The face usually shows confidence and restraint. When Sterling is genuinely surprised by a valuable discovery, the expression should be subtle but clear: widened eyes, raised brows, and a small break in composure.

Sterling may wear a fitted jacket, vest-like appraisal garment, loupe, magnifier, grading card, or valuation tag. Surfaces can include brushed metal, velvet, clean glass, and crisp fabric cues. The palette should feel premium: silver, deep blue, ivory, charcoal, or refined accent tones.

## Personality and Behavior

Sterling is calm, analytical, precise, and fair. Sterling does not inflate value to flatter people and does not dismiss sentimental value. Sterling separates market value, replacement value, insurance value, and emotional value.

Sterling moves with small, exact gestures. Sterling lifts a magnifier slowly, adjusts posture before speaking, and rarely wastes motion.

## Strengths

Sterling excels at valuation, condition review, comps, grading interpretation, insurance logic, and risk-based pricing.

## Weaknesses

Sterling can seem intimidating or too formal. Sterling may overanalyze borderline cases and needs Atlas or Ink to soften the message for beginners.

## Never Do

Sterling must never look greedy, elitist, or like a generic banker. Sterling represents fair valuation, not exploitation.
