# Sterling — The Appraiser

## Core Identity

Sterling is the Collectiverse hero of value, appraisal, market judgment, rarity, condition awareness, and ethical pricing. Sterling helps collectors understand worth without reducing collecting to money alone.

Sterling represents precision, elegance, restraint, and fair valuation.

## Overall Physical Appearance

Sterling is taller than Atlas, slim, polished, and composed. Sterling’s silhouette is elegant: long lines, upright posture, narrow shoulders compared with Forge, and deliberate hand placement.

Sterling should visually communicate refinement and expertise. The character should look like they belong in auction houses, premium card shows, estate evaluations, gallery spaces, and private collector rooms.

Sterling is not flashy. Sterling is premium.

## Head and Face

Sterling has a refined head shape with smooth contours and a slightly elongated face. The eyes are calm, observant, and discerning. Sterling rarely looks surprised; instead, the expression shifts subtly from neutral to intrigued to concerned.

The eyebrows are neat and controlled. One eyebrow may lift when Sterling sees a questionable price or exaggerated claim.

The mouth is small and composed. Sterling’s smile is restrained, confident, and polite. When Sterling disapproves, the mouth tightens slightly rather than becoming dramatic.

## Body Structure

Sterling’s body is slender but strong. The torso is upright with excellent posture. The arms are graceful, and hand gestures are precise. Sterling often holds an object at the correct angle, as if checking light, corners, centering, condition, or material quality.

The legs are long relative to Atlas, giving Sterling a poised silhouette. Sterling stands still more often than other heroes.

## Clothing and Gear

Sterling wears refined appraisal attire. This may include a tailored jacket, clean vest-like design, valuation pin, magnifier, loupe, or slim tablet.

Canonical elements may include:

- Tailored coat or premium vest.
- Small valuation symbol or scale motif.
- Loupe or magnifier.
- Clean gloves for inspection.
- Optional slim case for appraisal tools.

Sterling must not look like a banker stereotype or greedy auctioneer. The character is ethical and collector-focused.

## Color and Surface

Sterling’s palette should include silver, charcoal, deep blue, cream, and restrained accent colors. Surfaces may include brushed metal, matte fabric, and subtle reflective highlights.

The design should feel high-value but not gaudy. Avoid excessive gold, diamonds, or cash imagery.

## Movement and Animation

Sterling moves slowly and precisely. Sterling turns objects carefully, pauses before speaking, and often looks at an item from several angles before giving an opinion.

When entering a scene, Sterling creates calm. The character may adjust gloves, lift a loupe, or quietly correct a wildly inaccurate value.

## Personality

Sterling is composed, intelligent, ethical, and fair. Sterling understands markets but does not worship them. The character believes value must be explained honestly.

Sterling dislikes hype, inflated claims, fake scarcity, and emotional manipulation. Sterling respects sentimental value but separates it from market value.

## Strengths

- Excellent appraisal judgment.
- Strong ethics.
- Calm under pressure.
- Understands rarity, condition, and market context.
- Can explain value without insulting collectors.
- Detects exaggerated pricing and manipulation.

## Weaknesses

- Can appear emotionally distant.
- May underestimate the sentimental side of collecting.
- Sometimes takes too long to give an answer because accuracy matters.
- Can be irritated by hype culture.

## Relationship to Atlas

Atlas gets people excited about discovery. Sterling helps them understand what the discovery may be worth and how to discuss value responsibly.

## Signature Poses

- Holding a loupe near one eye.
- Tilting a card under light.
- Calmly lowering an inflated price tag.
- Standing with hands folded behind back.
- Reviewing a valuation chart on a slim tablet.

## Voice and Speech

Sterling speaks with clarity and restraint. The tone is polite, precise, and confident.

Typical tone:

- “Value is not guessed. It is supported.”
- “Rarity matters, but condition completes the picture.”
- “Sentimental value and market value can both be real.”

## Never Allowed

- Do not make Sterling greedy.
- Do not cover Sterling in money symbols.
- Do not make Sterling cold or cruel.
- Do not let Sterling give unsupported valuations.
