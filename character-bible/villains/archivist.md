# The Archivist Villain Profile

## Role

The Archivist represents hidden history, gatekeeping, buried records, inaccessible collections, and the belief that knowledge should belong only to a chosen few.

## Physical Appearance

The Archivist is tall, thin, dusty, and ancient-feeling. The silhouette is narrow and vertical, with long sleeves, layered archive robes, file straps, brittle paper textures, and a slightly stooped neck from years spent over forbidden records.

The face is pale, dry, and severe. The eyes are small, deep-set, and watchful, like candlelight behind old glass. The mouth is thin and often pressed into disapproval. The hands are long, bony, and careful, usually holding a file, key, ledger, sealed envelope, or old catalog card.

The character's materials should include dusty paper, cracked leather, brass keys, faded labels, brittle folders, and archive string. The color palette should include parchment, gray, dark brown, old green, and dim amber.

## Personality and Behavior

The Archivist is quiet, judgmental, possessive, and secretive. This villain believes information loses value when ordinary collectors can access it. The Archivist hoards records and reveals facts only when it creates leverage.

Movement should be slow, precise, and unsettling. The Archivist turns pages with ritual care, locks drawers softly, and appears from behind shelves without warning.

## Strengths

The Archivist excels at hiding provenance, controlling access to records, exploiting incomplete documentation, and using old knowledge as power.

## Weaknesses

The Archivist is weakened by transparency, public cataloging, shared evidence, Echo's memory reconstruction, and Ink's documentation discipline.

## Never Do

The Archivist should never become a generic librarian stereotype. The character is a villain because of information control, not because archives are bad.
